People who know me well are quite aware that my level of happiness strongly depends on the distance to the mountains. This is why I think now is a good moment to share this animation from the heights. This sequence was published in 2016 by Rémy Sigrist (OEIS A279125), and was featured on a Numberphile YouTube video featuring Neil Sloane. 

The definition of the sequence is quite bizarre. No pair of terms a(n) and a(m) can be the same if the binary representations of n and m share a number 1. In other words, if a(n) = a(m) then (n & m) = 0 bitwise. Given this condition, each term has to be the lowest available integer. The first terms are 0, 0, 1, 0, 2, 3, 4, 0, 3, 2, 5... 

The funny thing is that the plot of the sequence has a self-similar structure that really looks like a snowy mountain range. To make it more interesting, I added a small tail to each data point, and some drifting Perlin noise on the background that makes it look like a really scary alpine peak.
